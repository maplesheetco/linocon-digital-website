It's the first question almost every business owner asks us, and the honest answer is "it depends." That's not very helpful on its own, so here's what it actually depends on, with ballpark numbers you can plan around.

## The short answer

For most small businesses, a website falls into one of three bands:

| Option | Typical upfront cost | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DIY website builder | $0 to $500 | Testing an idea, very tight budgets |
| Freelancer or template build | $1,500 to $5,000 | Simple service businesses with a few pages |
| Agency or custom build | $5,000 to $25,000+ | Businesses that rely on the site for leads or sales |

These are typical ranges, not quotes. Where you land depends on the factors below.

## What drives the price up (or down)

**Number of pages.** A five-page site (home, services, about, contact, booking) costs far less than a 30-page site with a page for every service and location.

**Design.** Adapting a template is quicker than designing from scratch. A custom design takes more hours, but it's what makes you look different from the competitor down the street.

**Copywriting.** If you write your own text, you save money. If you want someone to write words that actually persuade people to call you, budget for it.

**Features.** Online booking, payments, a store, a member area, or connections to your CRM all add time. A contact form is cheap. A full online store is not.

**SEO setup.** A site that looks good but isn't set up for Google is like a shop with no sign. Proper page titles, descriptions, speed, and structure should be part of the build, not an afterthought.

## The ongoing costs nobody mentions

The build is only part of the bill. Plan for these every year:

- **Domain name:** about $15 to $30 a year.
- **Hosting:** $0 to $50 a month for most small business sites.
- **Email at your domain:** around $6 to $12 per person per month.
- **Maintenance and updates:** either your time or a monthly care plan.
- **Marketing:** SEO, content, and backlinks are what bring visitors once the site is live.

## Is a cheap website a false economy?

Not always. If you're just starting out, a simple DIY site is far better than nothing. The problem comes when a business relies on its website for leads but the site is slow, hard to use on a phone, or invisible on Google. Then the "cheap" site is quietly costing you customers every week.

A useful way to think about it: if one new customer is worth $1,000 to you, a site that brings in just one extra customer a month pays for itself quickly.

## How to get an accurate quote

Before you talk to anyone, write down:

1. What you want the website to do (get calls, take bookings, sell products).
2. The pages you think you need.
3. Two or three websites you like, and why.
4. Your rough budget.

With that, any good web designer can tell you quickly what's realistic.
