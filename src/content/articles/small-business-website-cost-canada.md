Most Canadian small businesses spend somewhere between **$2,500 and $8,000** for a professionally built website, with simple do-it-yourself sites costing far less and custom or e-commerce builds costing considerably more. There's no single price because "a website" can mean anything from a five-page brochure site to an online store connected to your inventory and accounting software.

This guide gives you realistic planning ranges in Canadian dollars, explains what drives the price up or down, and shows you how to judge whether a quote is fair for what your business actually needs.

## How much does a small business website cost in Canada?

Here are typical ranges you'll see from Canadian providers in 2026. They're planning guidance, not quotes, and prices are before GST/HST.

| Website type | Typical upfront cost (CAD) | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DIY website builder | $0 to $1,000, plus a $20 to $60 monthly subscription | Very small businesses testing an idea |
| Professional small business website | $2,500 to $8,000 | Service businesses that need credibility and enquiries |
| Custom or advanced website | $8,000 to $25,000+ | Businesses that need custom features or integrations |
| E-commerce website | $5,000 to $30,000+ | Businesses selling products online |

**DIY website builder.** You pick a template on a platform like Wix, Squarespace, or Shopify and build the site yourself. The cash cost is low, but your time isn't free, and the result depends on your design, writing, and technical skills.

**Professional small business website.** Usually five to fifteen pages (home, services, about, contact, and a page for each main service), a design tailored to your brand, mobile-friendly layouts, contact or booking forms, and basic SEO setup. This is the right fit for most trades, clinics, consultants, and local service businesses.

**Custom or advanced website.** Fully custom design and development, plus features like client portals, booking systems, multilingual content (English and French, for example), or connections to your CRM and other software.

**E-commerce website.** Selling online adds product pages, a cart and checkout, payment processing, shipping and tax settings, and often inventory management. The price depends heavily on how many products you have and how much of the setup is custom.

## What determines the cost of a website?

Two quotes for "a website" can differ by thousands of dollars because they include very different amounts of work. These are the main factors.

**Number of pages.** More pages mean more design, writing, and setup. A five-page site and a forty-page site are different projects.

**Design.** Adapting a quality template is faster than designing from a blank page. Custom design costs more, but it helps you stand out from competitors using the same themes.

**Functionality.** Contact forms are simple. Online booking, quote calculators, member areas, and search filters all add development time.

**Content.** Someone has to write the words and supply the photos. If you provide them, you save money. If your provider writes persuasive copy and sources images, budget for it.

**Integrations.** Connecting your site to tools like a CRM, email marketing platform, booking software, or accounting system adds setup and testing.

**SEO.** A site built with search in mind (clear page structure, fast loading, proper titles and descriptions, and a page for each service) costs a little more upfront but gives you a far better foundation for being found on Google.

**E-commerce.** Every product, variation, shipping rule, and payment option needs to be set up and tested.

**Customization.** The more your site departs from standard layouts and features, the more hours it takes to build and maintain.

## DIY website vs. professional website

A website builder can be a sensible starting point, especially when you're testing a new idea on a tight budget. Here's how the two options usually compare.

| | DIY website builder | Professional website |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront cost | Low | Higher |
| Your time | Often 20 to 60+ hours | A few hours for input and reviews |
| Design | Template-based | Tailored to your brand and customers |
| Technical setup | You handle domain, email, forms, and settings | Handled for you |
| SEO foundation | Basic, depends on what you know | Built in from the start |
| Ongoing maintenance | You | You, or a care plan with your provider |

The real question is what your time is worth and how much your business depends on the website. If most of your customers find you through referrals and the site is just a digital business card, DIY may be enough. If you need the website to generate enquiries, a professional build usually pays for itself faster.

## What should be included in a professional business website?

Whatever you pay, a professional small business website should include:

- **Essential pages:** home, services (ideally a page for each main service), about, and contact.
- **Mobile-friendly design:** most visitors will see your site on a phone first.
- **SSL security:** the padlock in the browser bar, which visitors and Google both expect.
- **Contact forms** that reliably reach your inbox, plus click-to-call on mobile.
- **Clear calls to action** on every page, such as "Request a quote" or "Book a consultation."
- **Analytics** so you can see how many people visit and what they do.
- **Basic SEO setup:** page titles, descriptions, headings, image text, and connection to Google Search Console.
- **Good performance:** fast loading on mobile connections, not just on office Wi-Fi.

If a quote leaves any of these out, ask why.

## Website costs people often forget

The build is only part of the cost of running a website. Plan for these as well:

- **Domain name:** around $15 to $30 a year for a .ca or .com.
- **Hosting:** roughly $10 to $60 a month for most small business sites.
- **Business email:** about $8 to $12 per user per month for Google Workspace or Microsoft 365.
- **Maintenance:** updates, backups, and security, either your time or a care plan, often $50 to $300 a month.
- **Copywriting:** if you need help writing persuasive pages.
- **Photography:** professional photos of your team, premises, or work make a big difference to trust.
- **Plugins and tools:** booking software, premium themes, or form tools can carry their own subscriptions.
- **SEO and marketing:** a website doesn't bring visitors on its own. Ongoing SEO, content, and backlinks help people find it.
- **Future updates:** new services, new photos, and design refreshes every few years.

Adding these up before you start avoids surprises later.

## How to choose a website provider

When you compare providers and proposals, check:

1. **Their portfolio.** Do they have live sites for businesses like yours, and do those sites load quickly on your phone?
2. **What's included.** Number of pages, copywriting, revisions, SEO setup, training, and launch support should all be written down.
3. **Who owns what.** You should own your domain, your content, and ideally your website, with admin access in your name.
4. **Ongoing costs.** Ask what hosting, maintenance, and future changes will cost after launch.
5. **Timeline.** A typical small business website takes about four to eight weeks, depending on how quickly content and feedback come in.
6. **Communication.** You should know who your contact is and how often you'll hear from them.
7. **Realistic promises.** Be wary of anyone who guarantees first-page Google rankings. No one can promise that.

The cheapest quote is rarely the best value. Compare what each proposal actually delivers, not just the total.

## Is a professional website worth the investment?

For most small businesses, yes, because a website affects four things that drive revenue:

- **Credibility.** People check your website before they call. An outdated or broken site quietly sends them to a competitor.
- **Lead generation.** A clear site with strong calls to action turns visitors into enquiries, even outside business hours.
- **Visibility.** A site with a solid SEO foundation gives you a chance to appear when local customers search for what you offer.
- **Long-term value.** A well-built site keeps working for years, while most advertising stops the moment you stop paying.

A simple way to think about it: if a new customer is worth $1,500 to your business, a website that brings in one or two extra customers a month pays back a professional build quickly.

## The bottom line

A small business website in Canada can cost anything from a few hundred dollars to tens of thousands. For most service businesses, a professional website in the $2,500 to $8,000 range is the practical middle ground. Rather than starting with price, start with what you need the website to do, list the features that support that, and compare providers on what they deliver.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a basic website cost in Canada?

A basic DIY site can cost under $1,000 a year using a website builder. A basic professionally built site of a few pages typically starts around $2,500.

### How long does it take to build a small business website?

Most small business websites take four to eight weeks from kickoff to launch. Larger custom or e-commerce projects can take several months.

### Do I have to pay for my website every month?

You'll usually pay ongoing costs for hosting, your domain, and business email. Maintenance and SEO are optional but recommended if you want the site to stay secure and keep bringing in customers.

### Does a new website include SEO?

It depends on the provider. A good build should include basic SEO setup (titles, descriptions, structure, speed, and Search Console). Ongoing SEO work, like content and backlinks, is usually a separate service.

### Can I build my own website and hire a professional later?

Yes. Many businesses start with a DIY site and upgrade when the business grows. Just make sure you own your domain from day one so you can move it later.

### Are website costs tax-deductible in Canada?

Website costs are generally a business expense, but how they're treated (expensed or depreciated) depends on your situation. Check with your accountant.
